Rotational Energy Levels

For a rigid rotor diatomic molecule:

$$E_J = hcBJ(J+1), \quad J = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$$

The rotational constant $B$ relates to the moment of inertia:

$$B = \frac{h}{8\pi^2 c I}, \quad I = \mu r_e^2$$

Selection Rule & Line Spacing

Rotational transitions obey $\Delta J = \pm 1$. Each line corresponds to $J \to J+1$:

$$\tilde{\nu}_{J\to J+1} = 2B(J+1)$$

Lines are equally spaced by $2B$, so measuring spacing gives $B$ and thus the bond length. A pure rotational spectrum requires a permanent dipole moment.

Boltzmann Populations

The population of level $J$ at temperature $T$ is:

$$\frac{N_J}{N} = \frac{(2J+1)\, e^{-hcBJ(J+1)/k_BT}}{q_{\text{rot}}}, \quad q_{\text{rot}} = \sum_J (2J+1)\, e^{-hcBJ(J+1)/k_BT}$$

The most populated level: $J_{\max} \approx \sqrt{k_BT / 2hcB} - \tfrac{1}{2}$.

Line intensities follow the population of the lower level, weighted by the line strength and by $\tilde{\nu}\,(1 - e^{-hc\tilde{\nu}/k_BT})$ (absorption minus stimulated emission), so the intensity maximum sits at somewhat higher $J$ than $J_{\max}$.

Determining Bond Lengths

From a measured $B$ (in cm⁻¹), the bond length is:

$$r_e = \sqrt{\frac{h}{8\pi^2 c \mu B}}$$

Heavy molecules (CO, NO) have small $B$ and dense line patterns; light hydrides (HF, HCl) have large $B$ and widely spaced lines — switch to the common axis scale to compare. Homonuclear N₂ has no microwave spectrum; its $B$ comes from rotational Raman spectroscopy.

Using $B_0$ gives the vibrationally averaged $r_0$; the equilibrium $r_e$ uses $B_e = B_0 + \alpha_e/2$.
